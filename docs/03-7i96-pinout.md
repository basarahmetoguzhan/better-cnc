## What this table is and is not

**It IS:** the driver's own label for each of the 51 IO pins, plus the function
each pin carries in the *stock Mesa `7i96d` bitfile* as modelled by pncconf.

**It is NOT:** a description of the Zhulong V2.0. We have no vendor documentation for
that board, and we now know from photographs that its connector set differs
substantially (see the banner above and
[04-zhulong-board-hardware.md](04-zhulong-board-hardware.md)).

Three independent things differ or may differ on our board:

1. **The connectors themselves.** OBSERVED: the Zhulong has six AXIS blocks, three
   encoder blocks, one five-way input block, two output blocks, an analog block and
   two Smart Serial jacks. It has **no DB25/P1**. The `TB1`/`TB2`/`TB3`/`P1` names
   below do not appear on its silkscreen at all.
2. **The function assignment.** Which FPGA pin is Step 0 vs Dir 0 vs an encoder input
   is decided entirely by the **bitfile in the board's flash**, not by the driver.
3. **The geometry.** Whether the Zhulong's IDROM even reports 3 ports × 17 pins is
   unknown. If it does not, and the board still calls itself `7I96`, the driver
   refuses to load — that is Outcome C in
   [02-board-bringup.md](02-board-bringup.md).

**Therefore: the "stock Mesa 7i96d function" column below describes hardware we do not
own.** It is retained for understanding the driver and the upstream docs, not for
wiring. Our board's real assignment comes out of `mesaflash --readhmid`, which prints
one `IO Pin NNN (label): function` line per pin. See
[02-board-bringup.md](02-board-bringup.md) step 5. Once you have the dump, record the
real mapping in [04-zhulong-board-hardware.md](04-zhulong-board-hardware.md) and the
deltas in this file's verification log.

The pin *labels* (column 4) are verified against source and will not change — the
driver hardcodes them for any board whose name matches `7I96` or `7I96S`.

**On the unrecognized-board fallback path this array is not used at all.** dmesg
instead prints `??-01, ??-14, ??-02, …` — DB25 numbering applied to all three ports.
The labels below will not appear, and the ones that do appear are wrong. See
[02-board-bringup.md](02-board-bringup.md) Outcome B for the mechanism. On that path,
**key everything off the IO number in column 1**, which is correct on both paths.

## Stock 7i96 vs. observed Zhulong V2.0 — why the bitfile must be different

Stock figures are the `7i96d` record established in
[00-upstream-reference-map.md](00-upstream-reference-map.md) §e
([private_data.py:1042-1043](../reference/linuxcnc/src/emc/usr_intf/pncconf/private_data.py#L1042-L1043)).
Zhulong figures are counted from photographs of the connectors — see
[04-zhulong-board-hardware.md](04-zhulong-board-hardware.md) for the full description
and the confidence rating of each observation.

| I/O resource | Stock Mesa 7i96 (`7i96d` bitfile) | Zhulong V2.0 (connectors OBSERVED) | Compatible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step/dir channels | **5** StepGen (TB1 ×4, TB2 ×1) | **6** — `AXIS 0`…`AXIS 5` | ❌ needs more |
| Encoder channels | **1** Encoder (TB2: A, B, Z) | **3** — `Encoder 0/1/2` | ❌ needs more |
| PWMGen | **0** | **≥1 INFERRED** — `0-10V + CW` analog block | ❌ needs more |
| Smart Serial ports | **1** port, 1 channel | **2** — `Smart Serial 0`, `Smart Serial 1` | ❌ needs more |
| Dedicated inputs | **11** (TB3-01…TB3-11) | **5** — `20 21 22 23 24` | fewer |
| Outputs | **6** SSR (TB3-13…TB3-24) | **2** — `OUT 1`, `OUT 2`, type UNVERIFIED | fewer |
| DB25 / P1 GPIO | **17** (IO 34–50) | **none — connector absent** | ❌ absent |
| Watchdog | 1 | UNVERIFIED | — |
| ClockLow / ClockHigh | 33 / 200 MHz | UNVERIFIED (50.000 MHz crystal OBSERVED) | — |

### The conclusion this forces

**The Zhulong cannot be running the stock `7i96d` bitfile.** The argument does not rely
on any uncertain observation:

- A bitfile that instantiates **5** StepGens cannot drive **6** axis connectors.
- A bitfile that instantiates **1** Encoder cannot serve **3** encoder connectors.
- A bitfile with **0** PWMGens cannot produce a 0–10 V analog output.

Any one of those alone settles it. All three together make it certain. The vendor
compiled their own HostMot2 configuration for this board.

**Three consequences that shape everything downstream:**

1. **The whole "stock function" column below is inapplicable to our hardware.** Not
   "probably shifted by a few pins" — describing a different device.
2. **A stock Mesa 7i96 bitfile is not merely unhelpful here, it is dangerous.** It would
   assign FPGA pins to functions the board's external circuitry is not wired for —
   driving an output into an optocoupler input, or an input pin onto a driven net. This
   is the concrete reason behind the "do not flash" rule in
   [02-board-bringup.md](02-board-bringup.md) Outcome C.
3. **Geometry is now an open question, not a formality.** Six axes × 2 + three encoders
   × 3 + 5 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 36 pins of committed function (arithmetic in
   [04-zhulong-board-hardware.md](04-zhulong-board-hardware.md)). That fits inside 51,
   so a 3 × 17 IDROM is *possible* — but the absent P1 means 17 stock pins go nowhere,
   and the vendor may well have declared fewer ports. If they did, **and** the board
   still reports the name `7I96`, the driver hard-aborts. That is Outcome C, and it is
   now a live possibility rather than a theoretical one.

## Connector ↔ IO number mapping

**(Stock board. Our board's mapping is unknown — see the banner.)**

Derived, not guessed. Two facts from source:

- `pins_per_connector = 17` and `num_ioport_connectors = 3`
  ([hm2_eth.c:1322-1323](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hm2_eth.c#L1322-L1323))
- `pin->port_num = i / hm2->idrom.port_width`
  ([pins.c:748](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/pins.c#L748))
  — so port number is simply the global IO index divided by 17.

That gives:

| Port | IO numbers | Connector, per `hm2_7i96_pin_names[]` |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | **IO 0 – IO 16** | TB3 (11 inputs + 6 SSR outputs) |
| 1 | **IO 17 – IO 33** | TB1 (8 pins) + TB2 (7 pins) + 2 internal pins |
| 2 | **IO 34 – IO 50** | P1 / DB25 (17 pins) |

Port 1 is the awkward one: it spans **two physical terminal blocks plus two pins
brought out nowhere**. TB1 gets IO 17–24, TB2 gets IO 25–31, and IO 32/33 are internal.

## ⚠ Source inconsistency — read this before trusting `ioport_connector_name[]`

The 7i96 branch sets connector names in an order that **contradicts** the pin-name
array. From [hm2_eth.c:1325-1337](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hm2_eth.c#L1325-L1337):

```c
// DB25, 17 pins used, IO 34 to IO 50
board->llio.ioport_connector_name[0] = "P1";

// terminal block, 8 pins used, Step & Dir 0-3
board->llio.ioport_connector_name[1] = "TB1";

// terminal block, 7 pins used, Step & Dir 4, Enc A, B, Z, serial Rx/Tx
board->llio.ioport_connector_name[2] = "TB2";

// terminal block, 11 inputs, 6 SSR outputs
board->llio.ioport_connector_name[3] = "TB3";
```

Index `[0]` corresponds to port 0 = IO 0–16. But the comment on that very line says
*"IO 34 to IO 50"*, and `hm2_7i96_pin_names[0]` is `"TB3-01"`. So:

- **The comment and the array agree** (P1 really is IO 34–50; TB3 really is IO 0–16).
- **The index does not.** `ioport_connector_name[]` is effectively in the wrong order:
  `[0]` should be TB3, `[1]` TB1/TB2, `[2]` P1.
- `[3] = "TB3"` is written but **never read** — `num_ioport_connectors` is 3, so only
  indices 0–2 are ever accessed
  ([hostmot2.c:1252](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hostmot2.c#L1252)).

**Is this harmful? No — and here is the proof.** Pin labelling takes the
`io_connector_pin_names` branch whenever that array is non-NULL, which it is for the
7i96 ([pins.c:878-885](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/pins.c#L878-L885)):

```c
if (hm2->llio->io_connector_pin_names == NULL) {
    snprintf(connector_pin_name, sizeof(connector_pin_name), "%s-%02d",
             hm2->llio->ioport_connector_name[pin->port_num], pin->port_pin);
} else {
    if (hm2->llio->io_connector_pin_names[i] == NULL) {
        continue;
    }
    snprintf(connector_pin_name, sizeof(connector_pin_name), "%s",
             hm2->llio->io_connector_pin_names[i]);
}
```

So `ioport_connector_name[]` is used only for a NULL/printability sanity check
([hostmot2.c:1244-1276](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hostmot2.c#L1244-L1276))
and never reaches the user for this board. **`hm2_7i96_pin_names[]` is authoritative.**

**Independent corroboration** from a second file in the same tree — pncconf's board
metadata lists the tabs in the *array's* order, not the `ioport_connector_name[]` order
([private_data.py:1359](../reference/linuxcnc/src/emc/usr_intf/pncconf/private_data.py#L1359)):

```python
'7i96': {'DRIVER':'hm2_eth','PINS_PER_CONNECTOR':17,'TOTAL_CONNECTORS':3,
         'TAB_NUMS':[1,2,3],'TAB_NAMES':['TB3','TB1/TB2','P1']},
```

`['TB3','TB1/TB2','P1']` matches the pin-name array exactly. That settles it.

### Two smaller source defects

- **IO 24 label uses a hyphen where every sibling uses a slash:** `"TB1-22-TB1-23"`
  instead of `"TB1-22/TB1-23"` ([hm2_eth.c:129](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hm2_eth.c#L129)).
  Cosmetic, but if you grep dmesg for `TB1-22/` you will find nothing.
- **The 7i96S shares this array** ([hm2_eth.c:1347](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hm2_eth.c#L1347))
  even though its TB2 comment adds *"Spindle Analog"*
  ([hm2_eth.c:1355](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hm2_eth.c#L1355))
  which the 7i96 comment does not have. So on a 7i96S at least one TB2 label is
  potentially misleading. Not our board — ours is the Spartan-6 (`6slx9tqg144`,
  [hm2_eth.c:1338](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/hm2_eth.c#L1338))
  variant — but worth knowing if you read 7i96S material online.

## Where the "stock Mesa 7i96d function" column comes from

There is **no 7i96 pinout in `reference/hostmot2-firmware`** — established in
[00-upstream-reference-map.md](00-upstream-reference-map.md) §e, that repo is
unmaintained since 2021 and predates the board entirely. So that column is
reconstructed from pncconf's internal model of the stock `7i96d` bitfile:
[private_data.py:1042-1055](../reference/linuxcnc/src/emc/usr_intf/pncconf/private_data.py#L1042-L1055).

Decoding key, from [pncconf.py:1170](../reference/linuxcnc/src/emc/usr_intf/pncconf/pncconf.py#L1170):

```python
pinconvertstep = {"STEP":_PD.STEPA, "DIR":_PD.STEPB, ...}
```

→ **`STEPA` = Step, `STEPB` = Dir.** Note the stock record lists `STEPB` *before*
`STEPA` for each channel, i.e. **Dir comes before Step** in IO order.

**A strong cross-check that the alignment is right:** pncconf puts `TXEN0` at TAB2
index 15, i.e. global IO 32. The pin-name array's IO 32 entry is `"internal"` with the
comment `/* SSerial TXEN */`. Two unrelated files, same pin, same function. That
pins the whole TAB2 ↔ IO 17–33 alignment to within one pin.

Caveat that remains: this describes **Mesa's** `7i96d`. The Zhulong's bitfile is its
own thing.

### Notes on the table

- **Pin pairs.** Most TB labels name **two** screw terminals (`TB1-02/TB1-03`). On a
  Mesa 7i96 those are the differential/paired terminals for one logical signal. Which
  of the pair is `+` and which is `-`, and whether the clone even wires them
  differentially, is **UNVERIFIED** — no vendor doc.
- **TB3-12 is absent.** The array jumps `TB3-11` → `TB3-13/TB3-14`. Terminal 12 is
  presumably a supply or common pin, not an FPGA IO. **UNVERIFIED.**
- **P1 numbering is not contiguous.** `P1-01`…`P1-09` then `P1-11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 21,
  23, 25` — odd only after 09. Consistent with an IDC header where the skipped pins are
  ground. **UNVERIFIED** for the clone.
- **IO 32 and IO 33 are not brought out to any terminal.** They are real FPGA IO pins
  that consume two of the 51, but there is nothing to wire.
- **Only 5 StepGens and 1 Encoder** in the stock configuration — which the Zhulong's
  six AXIS and three Encoder connectors already exceed. See the contrast table above.

## Relevant HAL names for these pins

These are driver-level names and **do apply to our board** — they come from the
HostMot2 modules, not from the pin map. Only the pin *numbers* you plug into them
differ.

With `<B>` = the board's HAL prefix (`hm2_7i96.0` on both the exact-match and the
`7I96*` fallback path — see [02-board-bringup.md](02-board-bringup.md)):

| Function | HAL name | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Any pin, as raw GPIO | `<B>.gpio.<III>.in` / `.in_not` / `.out` | [ioport.c:250,263,323](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/ioport.c#L250) |
| StepGen | `<B>.stepgen.NN.*` | [stepgen.c:883+](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/stepgen.c#L883) |
| Encoder | `<B>.encoder.NN.*` | [encoder.c](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/encoder.c) |
| SSR output | `<B>.ssr.NN.out-<MM>` , `<B>.ssr.NN.invert-<MM>` , `<B>.ssr.NN.rate` | [ssr.c:133,163,170](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/ssr.c#L133) |
| Board LEDs | `<B>.led.CR01` … `<B>.led.CR04` | [led.c:87](../reference/linuxcnc/src/hal/drivers/mesa-hostmot2/led.c#L87) |

`<III>` in the GPIO name is the **three-digit global IO number** from column 1 of the
table above (`%03d`), e.g. IO 5 → `hm2_7i96.0.gpio.005.in`. It is *not* per-connector.
